DOL starter

Direct-on-line means the motor is connected straight to the supply when the contactor closes. Two circuits do the job: a power circuit that carries the motor current, and a low-current control circuit that decides when the contactor closes.

Tap any component in either diagram to see what it is and why it is there.

Control circuit

Power circuit

How it runs

  1. Press Start. Current flows through Stop and the overload contact to coil K1.
  2. K1 pulls in. Its three main contacts close and the motor starts. Its auxiliary contact closes too.
  3. Release Start. The auxiliary contact keeps K1 energized. This is the seal-in.
  4. Press Stop, or trip the overload. The coil loses power, K1 drops out and the motor stops.

Star-delta starter

Starting a large motor direct-on-line draws a very large current. A star-delta starter first connects the windings in star, so each winding gets about 58% of the line voltage and the line current is about one third. When a timer runs out it switches the windings to delta for full voltage. It only suits a motor whose windings are rated for delta at your supply voltage.

Control circuit

Power circuit and windings

How it runs

  1. Press Start. K1 closes and the timer begins. K3 also closes, which joins the winding ends together: star.
  2. Watch the timer bar. The motor runs slower and gently while it accelerates in star.
  3. When the timer finishes, its contact opens the K3 rung and closes the K2 rung. K3 drops out and K2 closes: delta.
  4. Each of K3 and K2 has a normally closed contact in the other’s rung, so they can never be closed together. That would short the supply.

Forward-reverse control

To reverse a three-phase motor you swap any two of the three phases. Two contactors do it: F connects L1, L2, L3 straight through, and R swaps L1 and L3. If both closed together they would short the supply, so each coil rung contains a normally closed auxiliary contact of the other contactor. That is electrical interlocking.

Control circuit

Power connections

Try this

  1. Press Fwd. F pulls in and the motor turns one way.
  2. While it runs, hold Rev. Nothing happens, because F’s auxiliary contact in the Rev rung is open.
  3. Press Stop, then Rev. R pulls in, L1 and L3 are swapped, and the motor turns the other way.
  4. Trip the overload to watch both contactors drop out.

PLC ladder logic

A PLC replaces relay wiring with a program. Each rung is a row of contacts that must pass power to an output coil. The PLC repeats a scan cycle: read the inputs, solve the rungs from top to bottom, then update the outputs. Tap the inputs in the bar at the bottom to flip them, and edit any rung to see what changes.

Know your symbols

NO contact (examine if on)
Passes power when its address is ON. This is the ladder version of a Start button or a relay contact that closes.
NC contact (examine if off)
Passes power when its address is OFF. Use it for Stop. The program needs it as NC so that pressing Stop (input ON) cuts power.
Parallel branch
Two contacts side by side. Either one can pass power, which is OR logic. A branch with Start and the motor’s own output is the seal-in.
Output coil
Turns its address ON when power reaches it, and OFF when it does not.
Timer T0 (TON, 3 s)
Its contact turns ON after its rung has had power for 3 seconds without a break. It resets as soon as power is lost.
Scan order
Rungs are solved top to bottom, once per scan. A contact that reads an output defined lower down sees last scan’s value.
